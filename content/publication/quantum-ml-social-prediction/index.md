abstract: Quantum machine learning offers representational properties with theoretical alignment to prediction tasks prominent in the social and behavioral sciences. However, near-term quantum architectures have been evaluated predominantly on data geometries that may not reflect the ordinal measurement scales, structural sparsity, and distributional asymmetries commonly found in human behavioral and socio-political data, leaving this alignment largely unexplored in practice. This paper proposes an end-to-end quantum machine learning pipeline for social and behavioral classification tasks and evaluates four quantum classifiers against classical baselines on two established social science datasets, one drawn from psychometric survey instruments measuring relationship quality and the other from spatio-temporal records of armed conflict fatalities, under identical experimental constraints. By interrogating the resulting performance patterns, this study surfaces practical limitations, including the need for domain-specific encodings that preserve ordinal structure and imbalance-aware optimization that surfaces minority-class signal. This analysis further charts the engineering directions whose resolution would position quantum classifiers as genuinely competitive tools for modeling human behavioral dynamics in the social and behavioral sciences.
